Garmin watch workout .FIT

A structured workout with one step per split, each with its own target pace. The watch shows the target and alerts you when you drift outside it.

How to load it onto your watch
  1. Plug the watch into your computer with its USB cable.
  2. Open the watch's drive and copy the .fit file into the GARMIN/NewFiles folder. (On a Mac, newer watches need the free OpenMTP app to see the drive.)
  3. Unplug the watch. The plan shows up under Training → Workouts. Start it right before the gun.

GPS distance usually runs a little long over a race, so with automatic steps your splits will slowly drift ahead of the course mile markers. If you'd rather stay matched to the markers, choose On Lap press and press Lap at each split. The last step always lasts until you stop the workout. PacePro plans can't be imported directly. To build one in Garmin Connect instead, upload the course GPX there and create PacePro from it.

How the Planner Works

Grade adjustment. Each stretch of the course gets a pace multiplier based on its grade. Road-race climbs cost about 2.8% per 1% of grade. Past about 7% the cost rises much faster, following lab measurements adjusted to real runners' heart-rate data: a 10% climb is about 51% slower than flat at full effort, and a 15% climb about 89% slower. Descents help less than climbs hurt: the benefit is biggest around a −9% grade and shrinks on steeper ground. Past about −18% you're braking hard enough that the descent is no faster than flat, and very steep descents are slower than flat. For the research behind these numbers, see How Hills Affect Running Pace, Grade by Grade.

Hill effort. At 0% you run the same pace everywhere, regardless of terrain. At 100% you hold an even effort, so your pace changes by the full grade multiplier. Values in between blend the two. Most runners race well somewhere around 50–80%, because pushing uphill costs more than it gains back downhill.

Your goal stays fixed. The planner solves for the flat-ground pace that makes all the adjusted segments add up to exactly your goal time. The pacing-across-the-race slider then tilts that plan so you start slower or faster than the finish.

GPX elevation. GPS elevation is noisy, so uploaded tracks are smoothed before grades are calculated. Total gain may read a little lower than your watch shows. If your file measures slightly long or short, type the official distance and the course is scaled to match.

Grade adjustments are estimates based on typical runners. Your real response to hills depends on fitness, terrain, footing, weather, and how comfortable you are running downhill. Use this plan as a guide, not a guarantee. For informational purposes only.
