Course Overview

The Prague Marathon course climbs a total of 360 ft109 m and descends 350 ft108 m, finishing within 3 ft1 m of where it starts. With about 14 ftabout 2.6 m of climbing per milekilometer, it’s the 25th hilliest of the 49 marathons in our course library.

The first half climbs 207 ft63 m and drops 196 ft60 m. The second half climbs 149 ft45 m and drops 157 ft48 m.

Where the Hills Are

There are no real hills on this course. No stretch climbs more than 39 ft12 m, so you can run close to even pace the whole way. The short rises that do exist are folded into the splits below.

How to Pace the Prague Marathon

  • Even pacing works here. For a 3:30 goal every milekilometer stays within 3 seconds of the 8:01/mi4:59/km average, so a single goal pace on your watch is close enough.
  • Halfway: about 1:45:03 for a 3:30 finish and 2:00:03 for 4:00. The halves are close to even on this course.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Prague Marathon hilly?

Not very. It is mostly flat. The course climbs 109 m and descends 108 m over the full marathon, about 2.6 m of climbing per km. That makes it the 25th hilliest of the 49 marathons in our course library.

Where are the hills on the Prague Marathon course?

There are no real hills. No stretch of the course climbs more than 12 m, so you can run close to even pace the whole way.

What pace do I need for a 4-hour Prague Marathon?

A 4:00 marathon averages 5:41 per kilometer. Adjusted for this course at 60% hill effort, the kilometer splits range from 5:38 to 5:44, and halfway comes at about 2:00:03.

Is the Prague Marathon course net downhill?

No. It finishes within 1 m of its starting elevation.

What is the highest point on the Prague Marathon course?

The high point is 203 m above sea level, 16.2 km into the race. The low point is 186 m, at km 6.9.
