Course Overview

The Shanghai Marathon course climbs a total of 160 ft48 m and descends 150 ft46 m, finishing within 7 ft2 m of where it starts. With about 6 ftabout 1.1 m of climbing per milekilometer, it’s the 2nd flattest of the 49 marathons in our course library.

The first half climbs 42 ft13 m and drops 43 ft13 m. The second half climbs 113 ft34 m and drops 105 ft32 m.

Where the Hills Are

There are no real hills on this course. No stretch climbs more than 39 ft12 m, so you can run close to even pace the whole way. The short rises that do exist are folded into the splits below.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Shanghai Marathon hilly?

No, it is flat. The course climbs 48 m and descends 46 m over the full marathon, about 1.1 m of climbing per km. That makes it the 2nd flattest of the 49 marathons in our course library.

Where are the hills on the Shanghai Marathon course?

There are no real hills. No stretch of the course climbs more than 12 m, so you can run close to even pace the whole way.

What pace do I need for a 4-hour Shanghai Marathon?

A 4:00 marathon averages 5:41 per kilometer. Adjusted for this course at 60% hill effort, the kilometer splits range from 5:40 to 5:43, and halfway comes at about 1:59:57.

Is the Shanghai Marathon course net downhill?

No. It finishes within 2 m of its starting elevation.

What is the highest point on the Shanghai Marathon course?

The high point is 12 m above sea level, 26.7 km into the race. The low point is 0 m, at km 35.1.
