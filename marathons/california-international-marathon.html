Course Overview

The California International Marathon course climbs a total of 440 ft135 m and descends 770 ft235 m, finishing 328 ft100 m lower than it starts. With about 17 ftabout 3.2 m of climbing per milekilometer, it’s the 19th hilliest of the 49 marathons in our course library.

The first half climbs 319 ft97 m and drops 562 ft171 m. The second half climbs 121 ft37 m and drops 207 ft63 m.

Where the Hills Are

There are no real hills on this course. No stretch climbs more than 39 ft12 m, so you can run close to even pace the whole way. The short rises that do exist are folded into the splits below.

The biggest descents: miles 10.7–11.1km 17.2–17.8 (−74 ft−22 m).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the California International Marathon hilly?

Somewhat. It is rolling. The course climbs 440 ft and descends 770 ft over the full marathon, about 17 ft of climbing per mile. That makes it the 19th hilliest of the 49 marathons in our course library.

Where are the hills on the California International Marathon course?

There are no real hills. No stretch of the course climbs more than 39 ft, so you can run close to even pace the whole way.

What pace do I need for a 4-hour California International Marathon?

A 4:00 marathon averages 9:09 per mile. Adjusted for this course at 60% hill effort, the mile splits range from 9:03 to 9:16, and halfway comes at about 1:59:59.

Is the California International Marathon course net downhill?

Yes, it is net downhill. The finish is 328 ft lower than the start (357 ft at the start, 29 ft at the finish).

What is the highest point on the California International Marathon course?

The high point is 357 ft above sea level, at the start. The low point is 22 ft, at mile 24.2.
