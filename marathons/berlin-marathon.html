Course Overview

The Berlin Marathon course climbs a total of 220 ft68 m and descends 210 ft65 m, finishing within 10 ft3 m of where it starts. With about 8 ftabout 1.6 m of climbing per milekilometer, it’s the 12th flattest of the 49 marathons in our course library.

The flattest major marathon. World records have been set here multiple times.

The first half climbs 103 ft31 m and drops 92 ft28 m. The second half climbs 121 ft37 m and drops 122 ft37 m.

Where the Hills Are

There are no real hills on this course. No stretch climbs more than 39 ft12 m, so you can run close to even pace the whole way. The short rises that do exist are folded into the splits below.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Berlin Marathon hilly?

Not very. It is mostly flat. The course climbs 68 m and descends 65 m over the full marathon, about 1.6 m of climbing per km. That makes it the 12th flattest of the 49 marathons in our course library.

Where are the hills on the Berlin Marathon course?

There are no real hills. No stretch of the course climbs more than 12 m, so you can run close to even pace the whole way.

What pace do I need for a 4-hour Berlin Marathon?

A 4:00 marathon averages 5:41 per kilometer. Adjusted for this course at 60% hill effort, the kilometer splits range from 5:38 to 5:45, and halfway comes at about 2:00:00.

Is the Berlin Marathon course net downhill?

No. It finishes within 3 m of its starting elevation.

What is the highest point on the Berlin Marathon course?

The high point is 52 m above sea level, 27.9 km into the race. The low point is 29 m, at km 7.4.
